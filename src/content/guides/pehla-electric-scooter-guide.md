Thinking about your first electric scooter? Plenty of people in Chandigarh, Mohali or Panchkula who ride 20–40 km a day — students, homemakers, delivery workers — ask the same thing: "Which one should I get, what will it cost, and can I really ride it without a licence?" This guide answers exactly those questions — without praising any single brand.

---

## First, understand what a "no-licence scooter" actually means

There are two kinds of electric scooter on the market:

- **Low-speed scooter** — top speed up to 25 km/h, motor 250W or less. These need **no licence, no RTO registration, no road tax.**
- **High-speed scooter** — the ones that do 45–80+ km/h. These attract a driving licence, registration, road tax — the lot.

**Under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules (CMVR),** the low-speed exemption applies only when **both** conditions are met together — speed of 25 km/h or less **and** a motor of 250W or less. Low speed alone is not enough. Some scooters are capped at 25 km/h but carry a 350W or 500W motor — that is technically a registrable vehicle. This is the point where many buyers get caught out.

**Important for the Tricity:** Chandigarh is a Union Territory, Mohali is in Punjab, Panchkula in Haryana. The CMVR rules are identical in all three, but ground-level enforcement can differ slightly. So before you buy, **confirm once with your local RTO.**

---

## The real benefit of a low-speed scooter: the money you don't spend

Low-speed scooters do **not** get a government subsidy like PM E-DRIVE — that is only for registered, high-speed electric two-wheelers. But the low-speed advantage comes from the other direction — the costs you simply never pay:

| Cost | Low-speed scooter | Petrol scooter |
|---|---|---|
| Driving licence | Not required (age 16+ is enough) | Required |
| RTO registration | No | Yes |
| Road tax | No | Yes |
| Mandatory insurance | No* | Yes |

*Under CMVR, insurance is not legally mandatory for low-speed exempt scooters, though some read the MV Act differently. Confirm with your local RTO. Taking voluntary insurance is sensible.

**Saving on registration and road tax is a direct ₹1,500–3,000** — and you can ride the scooter the day you bring it home, with no paperwork.

## Home charging — what will it cost?

This is usually a Tricity buyer's very first question.

**Per the JERC tariff order** (effective 1 November 2025), Chandigarh's domestic electricity rate is tiered — around ₹2.75/unit at the lowest slab, around ₹4.80/unit in the middle slab, and up to ₹5.40/unit higher up. In Mohali, PSPCL's rate is around ₹5.40/unit for the first 300 units, and Panchkula (Haryana, UHBVN) sits in the same range.

**The running-cost maths:**

A low-speed electric scooter takes about **2 units** of electricity for a full charge. At a realistic ₹5/unit average:

> **One full charge ≈ ₹10**
> **At a 60 km range ≈ ₹0.17 per kilometre**

The same distance on a petrol scooter burns **₹150–200 of petrol.**

If you ride 30 km a day, that's roughly ₹150–180 a month in electricity — where petrol was probably costing you ₹1,500–2,000.

**For Chandigarh residents:** if your household uses little power and you're on the lowest slab, charging costs even less. But for an honest calculation, take the ₹5/unit average — it's slightly conservative but reliable.

## Battery: lead-acid or lithium?

This is the most important decision, and one many buyers skip.

### Lead-acid / gel battery
- **Cheaper** — lower upfront cost
- **Heavy** — adds to the scooter's weight
- Takes **7–12 hours** to charge
- Lower range — generally around 60 km
- Shorter life, may need replacing sooner

### Lithium-ion battery
- **Costlier** — roughly ₹10,000–15,000 more upfront
- **Lighter** — easier to ride
- Charges in just **4–5 hours**
- Better range — up to 80–120 km
- Longer life, cheaper in the long run

**Who needs what:**

- If you ride no more than 15–20 km a day and have time to charge overnight → lead-acid is fine
- If you ride 40–60 km a day or more, or charging time is short → **go lithium; the money is recovered over the long term**

## Which class to choose by daily distance

The low-speed market has several brands and models. We're not recommending any one model — but understanding the class matters. Shop by class and spec; the brands named are only examples of each class.

### If you ride 15–20 km a day — say college or the market
- An entry-level lead-acid scooter is enough
- A 60 km-range model is more than you need
- This is the **entry-commuter class** (~60–70 km, ₹35,000–45,000) — light, low seat height, good for new riders. Examples across brands: Komaki X-One, Kinetic Green Zing, Zelio Little Gracy

### If you ride 30–45 km a day — say office or delivery
- Choose a lithium-battery model
- A 70–85 km range will be comfortable
- This is the **everyday step-through class** (₹45,000–60,000), the all-round daily commuter. Examples across brands: Hero Electric Flash LX, Deltic Costa, Zelio Gracy i

### If you ride 50+ km a day — delivery worker, shopkeeper, or many round trips
- Look at the **long-range / work class** — 90–110 km real range, often a removable battery
- Typical price band ₹60,000–95,000. Examples across brands: Deltic Legion, Komaki XGT VP, Zelio Logix

> **Note:** the price bands above are indicative, not exact ex-showroom figures — they vary by lead-acid vs lithium variant and by your state (Chandigarh/Mohali/Panchkula). Confirm the exact ex-showroom price, the real range and the ≤250W motor rating at the showroom.

## Is buying on EMI sensible?

That's a personal call, but keep a few things in mind:

- A low-speed scooter's on-road cost = the showroom price (no registration, no road tax)
- Running cost is very low — ₹0.17/km — so even while paying EMIs you'll see a monthly saving
- If you're spending ₹1,500–2,000 a month on petrol now, a good scooter's EMI can be the same or less

---

## What to ask when buying in the Tricity

1. **What is the exact motor wattage?** — it should be 250W, not more
2. **What is the top speed?** — it must not exceed 25 km/h (to stay exempt)
3. **Is the battery lead-acid or lithium?** — and what is its warranty?
4. **Does the charger run on a home socket (5A/15A)?** — most low-speed scooters charge from a normal domestic socket
5. **Where is the nearest service centre?** — how many authorised centres are there in the Tricity?

---

## One thing that often gets missed

Some dealers sell "no-licence scooters" that actually have a motor above 250W. The speed is 25 km/h, but the motor is more powerful — such scooters can technically fall within the registration net. **CMVR requires both conditions together.** So get the motor wattage in writing from the dealer, and if in doubt, **confirm with your local RTO.**

---

## In short

- No licence, no RTO, no road tax — only on scooters that are genuinely 250W/25 km/h
- Running cost ≈ ₹0.17/km — a tenth of petrol
- A full charge ≈ ₹10, on a normal home socket
- For 40+ km a day choose a lithium battery; for shorter distances lead-acid works too
- PM E-DRIVE subsidy does not apply to low-speed scooters — it is only for registered high-speed vehicles
- Always confirm the motor wattage with the dealer, and ask your local RTO if needed
