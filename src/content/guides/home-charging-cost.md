You don't need a special charger or a special connection. A low-speed electric scooter charges from an ordinary 5A wall socket — the same one your phone uses — and the cost is small enough that most owners stop thinking about it. Here's exactly what "small" means.

## A full charge: one to two units

A typical low-speed scooter draws roughly **1.5–2 units (kWh)** for a full charge from empty. What those units cost depends on your domestic tariff slab.

In **Chandigarh**, the JERC domestic tariff (effective 1 November 2025) runs at:

- **₹2.80 per unit** for the first 100 units a month
- **₹3.75 per unit** for 101–200 units

So a ~2-unit charge costs roughly **₹6–8** at the lower slab and a little more as you climb — call it **₹10–16** in real, all-in terms once a small fixed charge and electricity duty are factored in.

---

## Per kilometre, and against petrol

That works out to about **₹0.25 per km** to run a low-speed EV on Chandigarh power. The same distance on a petrol scooter costs in the region of **₹2–3 per km**.

Put concretely: a full overnight charge of roughly **₹12–16** moves you about as far as **₹150–200** of petrol would. That gap is the whole running-cost case for an EV, and it compounds every single day.

---

## The hyper-local wrinkle: Chandigarh vs across the border

Here's a detail you won't find on a national spec sheet. Chandigarh's domestic power (₹2.80–3.75/unit) is **materially cheaper** than Punjab's, where domestic tariff sits around **₹5.40+/unit** for the first 300 units.

The practical upshot: the *same* scooter is cheaper to charge in Chandigarh than across the line in **Mohali** — and cheaper still than running petrol anywhere. If you split time across the Tricity, charge at the Chandigarh meter when you can.

---

## A worked example

Ride 30 km a day, every day:

- **EV:** ~₹7.50/day (30 km × ₹0.25) → about **₹225 a month**
- **Petrol:** ~₹60–90/day → **₹1,800–2,700 a month**

Even at the conservative end, that's well over a thousand rupees a month back in your pocket — before you count oil changes, servicing and the price of the fuel itself rising.

## The bottom line

Home charging a low-speed EV is cheap, simple and needs no infrastructure: a normal socket, one to two units a night, roughly ₹12–16 a full charge. On Chandigarh's tariff especially, the running cost is low enough that the petrol comparison stops being close.
