You don't need a special charger or a special connection. A low-speed electric scooter charges from an ordinary 5A wall socket — the same one your phone uses — and the cost is small enough that most owners stop thinking about it. Here's exactly what "small" means.

## A full charge: about two units

A typical low-speed scooter draws roughly **two units (kWh)** for a full charge from empty. What those units cost depends on your domestic tariff slab.

In **Chandigarh**, the JERC domestic tariff (effective 1 November 2025) is **tiered**:

- around **₹2.75 per unit** at the lowest slab
- around **₹4.80 per unit** for 151–400 units
- around **₹5.40 per unit** above 400 units

On top of the per-unit energy charge there's a small fixed charge (roughly ₹20–30 per kW per month) and an electricity duty. So rather than quote the cheapest slab, it's honest to work the running-cost maths on an **all-in rate of about ₹5 per unit** — a fair, slightly conservative average. Low-usage homes sitting on the lowest slab genuinely pay less.

At that all-in rate, a **\~2-unit charge costs roughly ₹10** (less if you're on the lowest slab).

---

## Per kilometre, and against petrol

That works out to about **₹0.17 per km** to run a low-speed EV — a \~₹10 charge spread over a roughly 60 km range. The same distance on a petrol scooter costs in the region of **₹2.5–3.3 per km**.

Put concretely: a full overnight charge of roughly **₹10** moves you about as far as **₹150–200** of petrol would. That gap is the whole running-cost case for an EV, and it compounds every single day.

---

## How the Tricity compares

Here's a detail you won't find on a national spec sheet. Chandigarh's domestic tariff is tiered, so a modest-use household on the lowest slab (\~₹2.75/unit) pays noticeably less than a heavy user climbing toward ₹5.40/unit.

Across the border, **Mohali** (PSPCL, Punjab) sits around **₹5.40/unit** for the first 300 units, and **Panchkula** (Haryana) is broadly comparable. So the three cities are not far apart on a typical bill — the real, ownable point is that Chandigarh's lower slabs reward light domestic use, not that it's dramatically cheaper than its neighbours. Either way, at roughly ₹5 per unit all-in the running cost stays a fraction of petrol.

---

## A worked example

Ride 30 km a day, every day:

- **EV:** \~₹5/day (30 km × ₹0.17) → about **₹150 a month**
- **Petrol:** \~₹60–90/day → **₹1,800–2,700 a month**

Even at the conservative end, that's well over a thousand rupees a month back in your pocket — before you count oil changes, servicing and the price of the fuel itself rising.

## The bottom line

Home charging a low-speed EV is cheap, simple and needs no infrastructure: a normal socket, about two units a night, roughly ₹10 a full charge at an all-in ₹5/unit. Across Chandigarh, Mohali and Panchkula alike, the running cost is low enough that the petrol comparison stops being close.
