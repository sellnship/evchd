A low-speed electric scooter is the rare vehicle you can buy in the morning and ride home the same afternoon — no licence, no registration, no number plate. But that exemption rests on **two** numbers, and getting one of them wrong quietly turns a "no-paperwork" scooter into a vehicle the RTO expects to see.

## The rule, exactly

Under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules (CMVR), an electric two-wheeler is exempt from registration and licensing only when it meets **both** of these conditions:

- a top speed of **25 km/h or less**, and
- a continuous-rated motor of **250 watts or less**.

Meet both and the scooter is not classified as a "motor vehicle" for registration. In practice that means **no driving licence, no RTO registration, no road tax, no number plate**, and no legally mandated insurance. The minimum age to ride is **16**.

Because there's no registration step, an exempt scooter is ready to ride the moment you buy it — your total on-road cost is simply the ex-showroom price.

---

## The catch most sellers won't mention

Speed alone is **not** enough. A scooter electronically capped at 25 km/h but fitted with a motor rated *above* 250W is still, in law, a registrable motor vehicle — licence, registration, road tax and all. The exemption checks **power and speed together**.

This is the single most common point that gets glossed over on a showroom floor. Before you treat any scooter as "no-licence", confirm the **continuous** motor rating in writing — not the peak figure — and that it is 250W or under. Among the low-speed models sold across the Tricity (the Zelio Gracy and Logix lines, for instance), the mainstream variants are built to the ≤25 km/h, ≤250W spec — but the only number that protects you is the one on *your* scooter's spec sheet.

---

## What you save by staying exempt

Skipping registration is worth roughly **₹1,500–3,000** in one-time RTO and associated costs, plus the time and paperwork. You also avoid road tax and the obligation to carry mandatory insurance.

A note on insurance: third-party cover is **not** legally mandated for a non-RTO low-speed scooter, and voluntary cover is available and worth considering. Interpretations vary, and on-ground enforcement differs, so treat insurance as a "confirm with your local RTO" question rather than a settled yes/no.

---

## Tricity nuance: one rule, three administrations

The CMVR is central law and applies **identically** across the region — but it's enforced by three different transport set-ups:

- **Chandigarh** — Union Territory
- **Mohali** — Punjab
- **Panchkula** — Haryana

Day-to-day enforcement can differ slightly between the UT, Punjab and Haryana, so if you're buying near a border or plan to ride across one regularly, confirm the current position with your **local RTO** before you rely on it.

## The bottom line

If — and only if — your scooter is genuinely **≤25 km/h and ≤250W continuous**, you need no licence and no registration, and you can ride it home today. If either number is over the line, it's a registrable vehicle and the full RTO process applies. Check both before you buy.
