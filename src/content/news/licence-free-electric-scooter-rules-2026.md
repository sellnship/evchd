Short answer: **yes.** As of June 2026, a low-speed electric two-wheeler remains exempt from a driving licence, RTO registration, road tax and a number plate under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules (CMVR) — **provided it meets both conditions at once**: a top speed of **25 km/h or less** AND a continuous-rated motor of **250W or less**. The minimum riding age is **16**.

## The catch that hasn't changed

Speed alone is not enough. A scooter electronically capped at 25 km/h but fitted with a motor rated **above 250W** is still, in law, a registrable motor vehicle — licence, registration and all. The exemption checks **power and speed together**. This is the single most common point mis-sold on a showroom floor, so before you treat any scooter as "no-licence", get the **continuous** motor rating (not the peak figure) in writing.

## Tricity status

The CMVR is central law and applies identically in Chandigarh (UT), Mohali (Punjab) and Panchkula (Haryana). The three are administered by different transport set-ups, so day-to-day enforcement can vary slightly — if you're buying near a border or riding across one regularly, confirm the current position with your **local RTO**.

## What it means for a Tricity low-speed EV buyer

- The exemption is **intact in 2026**: no licence, no RTO, no road tax, no plate — *if* both ≤25 km/h and ≤250W are met.
- Get the continuous motor wattage on the dealer's spec sheet; a 25 km/h scooter with a >250W motor is a registrable vehicle.
- Insurance is not legally mandated for an exempt scooter, but voluntary cover is worth considering — and on this, as on the exemption itself, confirm the current position with your local RTO.
